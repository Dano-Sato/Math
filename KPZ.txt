## Scalar KPZ Rigidity Conjecture (SKR)

Let \(T\) be a stationary ergodic finite-range scalar directed random metric on the plane. Assume, near an interior direction \(v\):

1. The deterministic limit shape is \(C^2\) and strictly curved.
2. The microscopic directed geometry is uniformly irreducible: there are no persistent hidden sectors/channels, and all admissible local states communicate within uniformly bounded distance.
3. Passage-time fluctuations are nondegenerate of KPZ order:
\[
0<
\liminf_{n\to\infty} n^{-2/3}\operatorname{Var}T(0,nv)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty} n^{-2/3}\operatorname{Var}T(0,nv)
<\infty.
\]

### Conjecture

After deterministic centering and affine \(1{:}2{:}3\) KPZ rescaling, the full directed metric locally around \(v\) converges in law to the directed landscape.

Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\text{finite-range locality}
+\text{scalar metric composition}
+\text{uniform irreducibility}
+\text{strict curvature}
+\chi=\frac13
\Longrightarrow
\text{directed landscape universality}.
}
\]

### Motivation / counterexamples already excluded

- Long-range endpoint cocycles can preserve \(1/3\) fluctuations but violate finite-range dependence.
- Two independent KPZ channels can yield non-DL limits such as \(\max(\mathcal L_1,\mathcal L_2)\), but either violate scalar composition after hiding the channel label or violate irreducibility.
- Interlaced parity/layer constructions satisfy locality and \(1/3\) fluctuations but have persistent microscopic sectors; uniform irreducibility removes them.
- Defect-line, flat-edge, and heavy-tailed constructions typically violate stationarity, curvature, or the KPZ exponent.

### Main attack target

Construct a stationary ergodic finite-range, uniformly irreducible scalar planar directed metric with smooth strictly curved shape and
\[
\operatorname{Var}T(0,nv)\asymp n^{2/3}
\]
whose scaling limit is not the directed landscape.

The likely weak point is that the single exponent \(\chi=1/3\) may be too weak to force the full KPZ fixed point / Airy structure.
