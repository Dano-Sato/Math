## Scalar KPZ Rigidity Conjecture

Let \(T\) be a stationary ergodic finite-range scalar directed random metric on the plane. Assume:

1. the deterministic limit shape is \(C^2\) and strictly curved near some interior direction \(v\);
2. passage-time fluctuations in direction \(v\) are nondegenerate of order \(n^{1/3}\), e.g.
\[
0<\liminf_{n\to\infty} n^{-2/3}\operatorname{Var}T(0,nv)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty} n^{-2/3}\operatorname{Var}T(0,nv)
<\infty.
\]

### Conjecture
After deterministic centering and affine \(1{:}2{:}3\) KPZ rescaling, the full directed metric in a neighborhood of \(v\) converges in law to the directed landscape.

Equivalently:
\[
\boxed{
\text{locality}+\text{scalar metric composition}+\text{curvature}+\chi=\frac13
\Longrightarrow
\text{directed landscape universality}.
}
\]

### Why the assumptions matter

Several obvious counterexamples fail precisely because of these hypotheses:

- Adding an \(H=1/3\) fractional-Brownian endpoint cocycle preserves \(1/3\) scaling and geodesics but violates finite-range dependence.
- Critical defect-line / BBP-type constructions break spatial stationarity or homogeneity.
- Two independent KPZ channels give a non-DL limit such as
\[
\max(\mathcal L_1,\mathcal L_2),
\]
while preserving \(1/3\) fluctuations, but after forgetting the channel label they fail the scalar metric composition law.
- Heavy-tailed or flat-edge models typically change the exponent or violate strict curvature.

Thus the conjectured rigidity mechanism is that scalar dynamic programming prevents persistent competition between distinct KPZ phases.

A useful attack target is therefore:

> Construct a stationary ergodic finite-range scalar planar directed metric with smooth strictly curved shape and nondegenerate \(n^{1/3}\) fluctuations whose scaling limit is not the directed landscape.

The most plausible remaining route is a finite-range, non-i.i.d. hidden-state environment that somehow preserves scalar composition while sustaining two competing \(n^{1/3}\)-scale phases.
