Asymptotic Screening Conjecture (ASC)
Let (T) be a spherically symmetric rooted tree with bounded branching sequence
[
b_0,b_1,b_2,\dots
]
where every vertex at level (n) has exactly (b_n) children.
Let
[
b:=\operatorname{br}(T)
]
be the branching number of (T), and let
[
a:=\sqrt b.
]
The radial adjacency operator of (T) is the Jacobi operator whose off-diagonal coefficients are
[
a_n=\sqrt{b_n}.
]
For a finite consecutive branching block
[
w=(b_j,b_{j+1},\dots,b_{j+m-1}),
]
define the finite weighted path matrix
[
K_b(w)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a & \sqrt{b_j} & & & \
\sqrt{b_j} & 0 & \sqrt{b_{j+1}} & & \
& \sqrt{b_{j+1}} & 0 & \ddots & \
& & \ddots & 0 & \sqrt{b_{j+m-1}}\
& & & \sqrt{b_{j+m-1}} & a
\end{pmatrix}.
]
Define the spectral excess of the block by
[
\delta_b(w)
:=
\Bigl[
\lambda_{\max}(K_b(w))-2\sqrt b
\Bigr]_+.
]
A block is called exactly screened if
[
\delta_b(w)=0.
]
The conjecture is:
[
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)=2\sqrt{\operatorname{br}(T)}
\iff
\exists, w_1,w_2,\dots
\text{ partitioning a tail of }(b_n)
\text{ such that }
\delta_b(w_k)\to0.
}
]
Equivalently:
A spherically symmetric tree has minimal possible essential spectral radius if and only if its branching tail can be decomposed into consecutive finite blocks that become asymptotically spectrally screened.

The stronger exact-screening version, which may hold under bounded integer branching, is
[
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)=2\sqrt b
\iff
(b_n)_{n\gg1}\in\mathcal C_b^\omega,
}
]
where
{w:\lambda_{\max}(K_b(w))\le2\sqrt b}.
]
Known/observed supporting structure
1. The general lower bound
[
\rho_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)\ge2\sqrt{\operatorname{br}(T)}
]
is expected/known in this setting.
2. If the tail is decomposed into exactly screened blocks, the quadratic forms of the blocks concatenate additively at their endpoints, strongly suggesting
[
\rho_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)\le2\sqrt b.
]
Together with the lower bound, this gives equality.
3. For periodic branching sequences, equality appears to force constant branching. If (b_n) is (k)-periodic, one gets via Floquet theory and AM-GM
2\sqrt{\operatorname{br}(T)},
]
with equality only when all (b_j) are equal.
4. A nontrivial exactly screened defect exists. For background (b=2), the defect block
[
(3,1)
]
is spectrally critical rather than supercritical; it does not raise the spectral edge above (2\sqrt2).
5. This shows that “branching surplus somewhere” is not enough to force spectral excess. Surplus can be compensated by nearby deficit.
6. Riccati form at threshold (2\sqrt b):
Set
[
\beta_n=\frac{b_n}{b}.
]
Then the threshold Riccati map is
[
x_{n+1}=2-\frac{\beta_n}{x_n}.
]
It satisfies
\frac{(x_n-1)^2+(\beta_n-1)}{x_n}.
}
]
This identity suggests that branching surplus/deficit and nonlinear Riccati displacement are the underlying screening mechanism.
7. Writing (x_n=D_n/D_{n-1}) gives the continuant recurrence
[
D_{n+1}=2D_n-\beta_nD_{n-1},
]
so the block-screening condition can also be expressed through positivity of appropriate threshold continuants / finite Jacobi matrices.
Main proof target
The difficult direction is
[
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)=2\sqrt b
\Longrightarrow
\text{there exists a consecutive tail partition }
w_1w_2\cdots
\text{ with }
\delta_b(w_k)\to0.
}
]
A possible route is:
- use Persson/right-limit/criticality theory to obtain arbitrarily far-out near-threshold finite intervals;
- strengthen this from a sparse family of good intervals to a partition covering the entire tail;
- show that any region which cannot be absorbed into an asymptotically screened block produces a right limit with spectral radius (>2\sqrt b), contradicting minimality.
Potential counterexamples to test include:
- sparse hierarchical defects,
- quasi-periodic branching,
- substitution sequences such as Fibonacci or Thue–Morse,
- blocks whose individual spectral excess tends to zero but whose concatenation might retain positive essential excess,
- sequences with long critical regions separated by short recurrent barriers.
The key question is whether global spectral criticality forces a consecutive finite near-critical block decomposition, rather than merely the existence of sparse null sequences.
